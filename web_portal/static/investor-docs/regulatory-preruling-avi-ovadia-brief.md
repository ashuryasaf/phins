## Why 12 October 2026 is the right day to ask

Three public facts set the calendar. None of them is a PHINS projection.

1. **The health-fund arrangement the Comptroller examined is temporary through December 2026.** The State Comptroller's special report on Israel's long-term-care insurance array (Tammuz 5786 / June 2026, part of the population-ageing report) records that, after the erosion of Clalit Health Services' insureds' fund, the insurer agreed to keep operating Clalit's long-term-care cover only until the end of 2026, and that the solution eventually reached is temporary until December 2026 only.
2. **On 4 October 2026 the Authority published the permanent-solution proposal** that replaces cross-generational subsidy with a built-in combination of personal accumulation and insurance. Contemporaneous reporting (Funder, Calcalist, TheMarker, Globes; ICE on 5 October) puts the health-fund book at about **5.2 million** insured people. This pack has not attached an Authority PDF of that publication. The 5.2 million figure is used only as those outlets report it.
3. **The meeting sits inside that window:** 12 October 2026, in Jerusalem, eight days after the publication and before the December 2026 expiry the Comptroller describes.

The ask that follows from those facts is narrow. PHINS is not proposing to operate the closed older group, to reinsure the insureds' funds, or to replace the National Insurance nursing benefit. The ask is a pre-ruling on a specified optional private contract for people who can be underwritten into the younger design the Authority has itself described.

## What the enclosed 4 December 2023 circular actually changed

The circular supplied with this briefing is **Insurance Circular 2023-1-11**, "Amendment of the consolidated circular — Gate 6, Chapter 3 — Long-term care insurance", dated 4 December 2023 (21 Kislev 5784), signed by **Amit Gal**, then acting Commissioner of the Capital Market, Insurance and Savings.

Its operative instruction is short. The 2016 health-fund long-term-care circular had required that, throughout the insurance period, the insurer bear **at least 20 percent** of the insurance risk on the **basic layer**. Circular 2023-1-11 **repeals that minimum**, so a health fund can negotiate the insurer's risk share. It applies to health-fund long-term-care contracts concluded or renewed from publication.

The State Comptroller (June 2026) records the consequence of that December 2023 circular and of the related stabilisation steps:

- The Authority reduced the monthly indemnity by about **10 percent, from 5,500 NIS to 5,000 NIS**, and cancelled the insurers' risk, so that **100 percent** of benefits are paid from the insureds' fund.
- After no insurer bid for Clalit's operator tender, the Authority changed the qualifying test **from difficulty in 3 of 6 ADL to difficulty in 4 of 6 ADL**, and cancelled the insurers' continuation obligation.
- Policies are still marketed in a way that can lead the public to think private insurers bear the risk. After the December 2023 circular, they do not. The insureds bear it. The insurer is described by the Comptroller as managing the fund for a fee.
- At young ages the expected result is negative (annual premiums exceed expected annual receipts). At older ages it is positive. The Comptroller calls this a material cross-subsidy, and states that if a large enough share of younger insureds leave, the funds empty.
- Fund balances are not published to the public.

Funder's 4 October 2026 account, attributing the 2023–2024 steps to the Authority, describes a reduction **from about 6,100 NIS to 5,000 NIS**, an update of ADL definitions, and a higher action threshold, and says the Authority treats those steps as **temporary**. This brief does not average 6,100 and 5,500. Both statements are cited. The direction is the same: a lower indemnity, a tighter ADL test, and insurer risk removed from the basic layer.

That is the model on the enclosed circular. It is the model the Authority, on 4 October 2026, described as structurally unstable over time.

## What was published on 4 October 2026 — and what was not decided

Funder (4 October 2026, Ronit Morgenstern), writing up the Authority's publication, states the Authority's account of the current model in these terms: a uniform mutual policy at the health funds, operated by insurers who do not bear the risk; the risk sits entirely on the mutual insureds' fund; the product is voluntary and depends on cross-generational subsidy; there is no central balancing mechanism, so fund size and premium differ across health funds; regulation is split; transparency is weak; policy terms have been changed repeatedly. The Authority's proposed permanent solution is a **built-in combination of insurance and savings**, analogous to the pension system, for about **5.2 million** existing health-fund insureds.

The same account sets the cohort rule without printing an integer:

- An **age threshold will be set**.
- Above it, insureds are a **closed group**. Their rights and continuity of cover stay on the **existing insurance structure**.
- Below it, insureds are spread across insurers by **competitive tender**, with **long-term accumulation** and stability of premium and cover.
- About **85 percent** of long-term-care claims occur **above age 70**, and the chance of becoming nursing-dependent rises from that age.
- Care cost is described as about **10,000 NIS a month at home** and about **15,000 NIS a month in an institution**, for an average spell of about **40 months**.
- Legislation is required.

Two corrections belong in the spoken brief, because they are easy to overstate.

**The age cut is not 50, and it is not yet a statute.** A December 2025 staff outline reported by Bizportal discussed a band of roughly under 50 to 60. That article predates the 4 October 2026 publication and is not the publication. Calcalist is the outlet that prints **55**. Funder, Globes and ICE say the integer will be set. The question for Mr. Ovadia is which integer the Authority will defend, and whether 55 (Calcalist) is the working figure. PHINS does not need him to adopt 50. The filed contract is issuable below age 65. The disability layer in the filing attaches from age 3. There is no issue cap at 50.

**The proposal is not destined, and it is not in force.** TheMarker and Calcalist both report that the Finance Ministry side does not share the Authority's urgency. Calcalist reports that the Budget Division considers the health-fund model to have stabilised after the recent cuts. Any pre-ruling conversation should acknowledge that disagreement rather than speak as if the Knesset had already passed the split.

Clalit's book is stated two ways. The Comptroller (June 2026) says Clalit insures **about 2.5 million** people in this cover. Globes, reporting the 2026 book, says **about 2.7 million**. Both figures are left in the record. Approved health-fund long-term-care claims, on the Comptroller's series, rose from **8,195 to 20,711** between 2012 and 2023.

The National Insurance Institute (Bituah Leumi) nursing benefit is the public layer. It is not the health-fund policy, and it is not the PHINS contract. The Comptroller's finding is that the public layer and the health-fund layer are both under long-term strain, for different reasons.

The honest sentence for the room is this. The Institute's own actuarial path, as examined by the Comptroller, brings the reserve fund to zero in **2035**, with a 2026 deficit budget of about **10 billion NIS** already in the audited record, and with a Treasury warning that statutory payment capacity can come under pressure from **2029**. That is a negative actuarial balance that worsens because long-term nursing settlements have grown far beyond the 2018 estimate. It is not a finding that the Institute is insolvent in October 2026, and it is not a finding that citizens who meet a 3+ ADL test have no benefit today. The nursing benefit still exists. The health-fund indemnity still exists, on a tighter 4-of-6 test, through a temporary arrangement the Comptroller dates to December 2026. The Comptroller's forward sentence is that, looking at the expected growth in the number of nursing-dependent elderly, there is a concern that the long-term-care array does not have a sustainable ability to provide what residents need when they become nursing-dependent.

The household gap sits beside that public finding. On the Authority's cost figures as reported by Funder (about 10,000 NIS at home and about 15,000 NIS in an institution), a 5,000 NIS health-fund indemnity — the Comptroller's post-cut monthly figure — leaves a large residual with the family for a spell of about 40 months. Globes reports that the savings model is aimed at reproducing about 5,000 / 10,000 for up to five years, which still sits below the 10,000 / 15,000 cost pair. PHINS does not adopt those costs as its own pricing assumptions.

## Where the filed PHINS contract fits

The contract in the Israel regulatory application memorandum (Hebrew canonical, Draft 3.1; English companion; Section 15 is the 12 October 2026 supplement and leaves Sections 1–5 unchanged) is a **pure-risk** life policy. It has **no savings component, no cash value, and no surrender value**. An optional savings add-on exists on the platform and is **excluded from the filing**. If it is offered in Israel it is to be filed separately through a properly licensed entity. That exclusion is an advantage in this meeting, because the Authority is now talking about savings and insurance together, and the filing already refuses to hide savings inside the risk policy.

| Filed term (Draft 3.1) | What it is |
|---|---|
| Life sum | Face amount F below age 65; F divided by 4 from age 65. Maximum face below 65 is 1,000,000 NIS |
| Disability sum | A single lump sum. Below 65 it is life divided by 4, so F divided by 4 (contract ratio 1:4), capped at 250,000 NIS. From 65 it equals the stepped-down life sum (contract ratio 1:1 at that reduced sum) |
| Worked example | F = 500,000 NIS. Below 65: life 500,000, disability 125,000. From 65: life 125,000, disability 125,000 |
| Trigger | Permanent inability to perform at least 3 Activities of Daily Living, or an equivalent cognitive impairment, confirmed by medical and functional assessment. The paid benefit is binary: once the trigger is confirmed, the full disability sum is paid. Internal scores do not grade the payment |
| After a claim | The policy may continue. Below 65 the life sum becomes 0.75 times F from the next premium due date. The disability layer ends with its single payment |
| Pricing | One deterministic pricing kernel. The memorandum states that the rate tables are the platform's central tables version V2.0. The Authority's published long-term-care experience tables (annexed to the consolidated circular, Gate 6, Part 3, December 2023 amendment) are a benchmark and a reasonableness reference. They are not copied as the price |
| Who bears insurance risk | A licensed insurer and/or an approved reinsurance structure. PHINS is the technology and administration layer |

The ADL difference must be said aloud. The health-fund basic layer, on the Comptroller's account, was tightened **from 3 of 6 ADL to 4 of 6 ADL**. The filed PHINS trigger remains **permanent 3+ ADL** (or cognitive impairment) and pays a lump sum, not a five-year indemnity. That is a different promise from the tightened health-fund indemnity. It is also the promise the memorandum already asks the Authority to classify as life insurance with a disability mechanism, with a pre-ruling if the Authority reads the layer as a health or long-term-care class.

The savings difference must be said aloud as well. The Authority's younger-cohort proposal, as reported, is personal accumulation plus an insurance component, with the insurer bearing risk. The filed PHINS policy is the insurance component only. The platform's savings add-on is the separate piece, and the counterparties for that piece remain TBA. Asking the Authority to treat them as one product would contradict the filing.

## The pre-ruling we are in a position to request

The memorandum itself says it is a drafting framework and not a substitute for a pre-ruling. Section 40 of the Supervision of Financial Services (Insurance) Law, 5741-1981, is the approval gate the memorandum is built for. The filing list already specified there — appointed-actuary pricing report, morbidity and mortality by age, lapse, expense and commission, reinsurance and solvency, sensitivity cases, the consumer premium table, and a documented explanation of any deviation from the Authority's long-term-care benchmark — is the submission, not a new list invented for this meeting.

What changed on 4 October 2026 is the reason the classification question is now urgent, not the contract.

Ask Mr. Ovadia for five decisions, each one something the platform can already show:

1. **Classification.** A pre-ruling that the Draft 3.1 contract is life insurance with a lifelong permanent-disability mechanism under Section 40, and that the permanent 3+ ADL lump sum does not by itself reclassify the policy as health-fund group long-term care.
2. **Risk and the functional MGA.** Confirmation that PHINS may perform underwriting, billing, claims administration and actuarial support as the managerial and operational bridge, while claims reserves and final adjudication stay on a designated Israeli insurance company, and while the carrier's appointed actuary signs the basis. Every assumption change on the platform is versioned. AI recommends; the ledger decides; a person supervises.
3. **Savings, separately.** Direction on the licence, the entity, and the sequence if PHINS later files the savings add-on for the younger cohort the Authority has described. The add-on is not part of this pre-ruling. Counterparties are TBA. No counterparty is named in this brief.
4. **Perimeter.** Confirmation that this pre-ruling does not appoint PHINS to operate the closed older group, the insureds' funds, or the National Insurance nursing benefit. Those books stay where the statute puts them.
5. **Path and clock.** The submission order, the open conditions, named contacts, and a date for the next meeting. The Comptroller's December 2026 horizon is the reason to ask for a clock. It is not a reason to claim the Authority has already chosen PHINS.

Distribution (direct digital sales, collaborations, licensed agents), adjustable actuarial methods, and the later option to expand to other markets and become an insurance company stay on the agenda as future steps. They are not the decision this meeting has to finish.

## Functional MGA — who holds what

The structure we ask the Authority to pre-rule: PHINS runs the operating
functions; the designated Israeli insurance company carries the risk; an
investment company carries the savings add-on risk (counterparties TBA).

| Function | PHINS (functional MGA) | Counterparty |
|---|---|---|
| Underwriting | AI severity underwriting within carrier-approved guidelines; humans on flagged edge cases. | Designated Israeli insurance company approves guidelines and holds the risk. |
| Billing | Automated billing, auto-pay and dunning on the platform ledger. | Premium flows reconciled to the carrier; savings component to the investment company (TBA). |
| Claims | AI triage + fraud detection; human adjudication on complex or contested claims. | Claims reserves stay on the carrier; carrier retains final adjudication authority. |
| Actuary | Adjustable pricing / reserving models; every assumption change versioned and ledger-logged. | Carrier's appointed actuary signs off; Authority has audit visibility. |
| Savings add-on | Platform administration and unified customer view. Excluded from the Section 40 filing. | Investment company (TBA) carries the savings add-on risk under its own licence. |

**So what:** PHINS is the managerial and operational layer — the risk stays
on a licensed carrier — so the pre-ruling can approve the platform posture
without transferring insurance risk to an unlicensed entity. The future
insurance-company option is a separate, staged decision. The same separation
is what lets the Authority look at a pure-risk 3+ ADL contract now, and at a
savings add-on later, which is the sequence the 4 October proposal itself
implies for any private product offered to the younger cohort.
